The host city

Why Verona?

For the first time in the history of EYP Italy (PEG), a Regional Session comes to Verona: a city shaped by history, craftsmanship and exchange, the ideal stage for weaving local traditions into Europe's future.

The session's motto, "Crafting Unity: Weaving Local Traditions into Europe's Future," speaks directly to Verona: an event that celebrates local heritage while staying true to the European outlook of every EYP session. We want participants to discover and share cultures and traditions, weaving places, countries and customs into a tapestry of memories and hopes for the future.

The session builds awareness of European citizenship, sparks debate on the questions facing European democracy, and strengthens participants' English communication skills. Locally, it's a chance to deepen ties with Verona and its institutions — a meeting point open to everyone, where local traditions are rediscovered through a European, pluralist lens.

Essential places

Where to go

Verona's historic centre is compact, mostly flat and best explored on foot: the main sights sit within about 15–20 minutes of each other, inside a bend of the Adige river.

  • Arena & Piazza Bra — the Roman amphitheatre (1st century AD) that still hosts open-air opera every summer, on the city's biggest square. About 15 minutes on foot from Porta Nuova station.
  • Juliet's House — the courtyard with the famous balcony and bronze statue. It gets very crowded, so go early.
  • Piazza delle Erbe & Torre dei Lamberti — the market square on the old Roman forum. Climb the 84-metre tower (368 steps, or take the lift) for a 360° view of the rooftops.
  • Piazza dei Signori — the elegant square of the Scaliger lords, just behind Piazza delle Erbe.
  • Ponte Pietra & Roman Theatre — the Roman-origin bridge over the Adige; climb up to Castel San Pietro for the best sunset view.
  • Castelvecchio — the 14th-century fortress-museum of the Scaliger family (della Scala), whose most famous lord, Cangrande I, gives his name to one of our host schools.
  • Giardino Giusti & San Zeno — a Renaissance garden with cypress avenues and a labyrinth, and one of Italy's finest Romanesque basilicas.

Local traditions

What to try

Verona's kitchen is Veneto comfort food: rice, fresh pasta, polenta, slow-cooked meat and plenty of sweets. Ask for the local specialities:

  • Risotto all'Amarone — creamy Vialone Nano rice cooked with Amarone, the great red wine of Valpolicella.
  • Tortellini di Valeggio — thin hand-folded pasta with a meat filling from nearby Valeggio sul Mincio, served with butter and sage or in broth.
  • Bollito con pearà — boiled meats with pearà, a peppery sauce of breadcrumbs and bone marrow. A Verona classic.
  • Pastissada de caval — a slow-cooked horse-meat stew in red wine, served with polenta. One for the adventurous!
  • Pandoro — the golden, star-shaped cake that was born in Verona, and the sweet symbol of the city's festive season.

Verona also keeps centuries-old craft traditions alive, from marble-working to printing: look down at the pink Rosso Verona marble paving many of its streets and squares.

Good to know

Practical tips

A few things that make the days easier:

  • Getting around — from Porta Nuova station it is about 15 minutes on foot to Piazza Bra along Corso Porta Nuova, or take a city bus. Buy your bus ticket before boarding (tobacconists, ticket offices or the official app) and validate it on board.
  • Shoes — comfortable ones with good grip: the historic centre is paved with cobblestones and polished marble that gets slippery when wet.
  • Weather — mild and often rainy in October (roughly 10–18°C): bring layers and a light waterproof jacket. Live forecast ↗
  • Traffic zone — most of the centre is a restricted traffic zone (ZTL), so don't plan on driving into it.
  • Food & water — tap water is safe to drink, so bring a reusable bottle. Dinner is usually served from about 19:30, and some places charge a small cover (coperto).
  • Safety — keep an eye on your belongings around busy spots like Piazza Bra and Juliet's House. In an emergency call 112, the EU-wide number; pharmacies are marked with a green cross.
  • We're here — the Event Safe Persons are reachable throughout the session.

Why we do this

Vision & goals

RS Verona 2026 pursues a threefold goal: to nurture active citizenship, to support personal growth, and to build a habit of dialogue and democratic debate.

Goal 01

Active citizenship

Through the activities of the European Youth Parliament we want young people to care about public life: to notice the challenges around us, to see how institutions work to address them, and to start thinking about problems actively.

Goal 02

Personal growth

Our simulations give hundreds of participants the chance to practise teamwork and public speaking, and to test themselves alongside new people outside the school setting. Staying in the network opens the door to new careers and to meeting older members already established in a wide range of fields.

Goal 03

Dialogue & democracy

Debate is at the heart of every session: learning to listen to views different from your own, to defend your ideas with arguments, and to work towards a shared position — hands-on training in openness to dialogue and democratic practice.
