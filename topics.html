The academic programme

Topics & academics

Eight committees, eight European challenges. Across the session each delegate researches, debates and drafts a resolution on one of the topics below, guided by the Academic Team.

Committees

The eight topics

Committee names follow the European Parliament's abbreviations. Titles are the session's own — the questions are what delegates will answer.

AGRIAgriculture & Rural Development

Lett-us use the land

With nearly 1 in 3 EU farmers over 65 and only 11% under 40 — alongside rising land prices, urban sprawl and farmland concentrating in large agribusiness holdings — access to land has become the biggest barrier for young farmers entering the sector. How can the EU ensure young farmers have access to land to grow crops and promote family farming and local food systems?

CRIMOrganised Crime

High Stakes

Europe's drug market is expanding fast, valued at over €30 billion a year and fuelling organised crime, with record seizures reflecting rising demand. As trafficking networks grow more sophisticated and cross-border, how can the EU strengthen its response to drug trafficking while safeguarding fundamental rights?

CULTCulture & Education

Excuse my French

According to UNESCO's Atlas of the World's Languages in Danger, 128 languages within the EU are considered endangered. How can the EU secure the preservation and promotion of regional and minority languages while also ensuring cultural integration within the Member States?

ITREIndustry, Research & Energy

Going with the Flow

Hydroelectric plants generate 30% of all renewable energy in the EU, yet their potential is held back by ageing infrastructure and tightening environmental constraints. How can the EU modernise and expand its hydropower sector in a way that is both sustainable and fit for the green energy transition?

JURILegal Affairs

Cutting the strings

With corporations spending €380 million a year lobbying the EU, and watchdog organisations speaking out against a lack of transparency in some European institutions, concerns over corporate influence on policy-making keep growing. How should the EU work with outside organisations to create a Union where democratic processes are respected and valued?

LIBECivil Liberties, Justice & Home Affairs

Swipe Fatigue

As concerns grow over digital dependency and excessive screen time on young people's mental health and social development, these habits increasingly shape how younger generations interact with the world around them. How can the EU promote healthier digital habits while encouraging meaningful engagement with local communities, cultures and traditions?

REGIRegional Development

Cultural Renaissance

As many European regions face economic decline, rural depopulation and the loss of traditional cultural practices, local arts and culture are increasingly promoted as tools for regional revitalisation. How can the EU use local arts and culture to revitalise regions without compromising sustainability and cultural authenticity?

SEDESecurity & Defence

Standing Guard

As global conflicts escalate and the reliability of long-standing alliances such as NATO comes into question, the EU finds itself exposed by decades of underinvestment in defence and over-reliance on external security guarantees. How can the EU strengthen its shared defence policy and foster cooperation between Member States to ensure its own security and strategic autonomy?

What it builds towards

The resolution

Every committee's work converges into one document — a resolution. It opens with an aim statement setting out the "political" line the committee takes on its topic, followed by because clauses (the reasoning) and by clauses (the measures proposed). The General Assembly then debates and votes on it.

The stance

Aim statement

A single opening line that states the position — the "political" line — the committee adopts on its topic.

The reasoning

Because clauses

Introductory clauses: the context, evidence and reasoning that justify the resolution.

The measures

By clauses

Resolving clauses: the concrete actions the committee proposes to address the problem.
